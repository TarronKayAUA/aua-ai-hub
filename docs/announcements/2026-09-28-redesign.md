# The AUA AI Hub Has a New Design

*September 28, 2026*

The AUA AI Hub has been redesigned. Most pages say what they said before; what has changed is how they are arranged and how you reach them, and those changes were made against measurements rather than by eye alone.

What you will notice:

- **Navigation in one place.** The menu columns down the sides of the page are gone. Every page has one control in the bottom-left corner: Browse opens a map of the section you are in, and On This Page jumps to any part of the page. After a few seconds it folds into a small compass labeled Navigate; hover over it or tap it to open it again.
- **The full width of the screen.** Reading pages now use the whole screen on a desktop, with sections side by side in outlined panels instead of one narrow column, and every line kept to a comfortable reading length.
- **Colors that mean something.** Each color stands for one kind of page everywhere on the site (tools, prompts, guides, lessons, benchmarks, news and events, governance), and a small color key appears wherever colors are used.
- **Pictures of Antigua.** The main pages carry scenes of the island drawn in code from photographs, under the night sky as it was over Antigua on a May evening. They are decoration only, and they are left out on phones.

## Why

The site had grown to more than a hundred pages, and its navigation had grown with it: one menu tab held 26 of the site's 64 entries. On a desktop screen, about half of every reading page was empty space, and on a phone some pages ran to more than sixteen screens of scrolling.

## How It Was Done

The changes were tested against how the site is used:

- **Everyday tasks.** Artificial intelligence (AI) agents stood in for visitors (a first-semester student on a phone, a student on rotation, a course director, an administrator and others) and worked through tasks on the old site and on each proposed design, with the clicks, the backtracking and the space taken from reading all counted. Some tasks were held back from the designers, so no design could simply be built around the test.
- **Designs compared on one measure.** Several designs were built in parallel and measured by the same script, above all for how much of each screen is left blank and how long each line is.
- **An independent reviewer.** A second AI model, from a different company, reviewed the designs, and its critique changed the outcome more than once. The final choices were made by the site's maintainer.

Some of the results:

| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Blank share of a reading page on a desktop screen | 51% | 19% |
| Prompt Library for students, screens of scrolling on a phone | 16.1 | 5.6 |
| Home page, screens of scrolling on a phone | 6.7 | 4.5 |

The test visitors were AI agents, not people, so the real test is your use of the site. The full account of how the redesign was done, including what did not work and what was overruled, is in the Project Write-Up [When Every Design Passes](../worked-examples/redesign.md).
